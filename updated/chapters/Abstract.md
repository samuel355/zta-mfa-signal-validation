# Abstract

The widespread adoption of remote and hybrid work has exposed critical limitations in perimeter-based security. Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) strengthen access control, but their effectiveness is undermined by unreliable contextual signals, siloed Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems, usability challenges, and inadequate privacy safeguards.

This study proposes and evaluates a multi-source context validation framework that strengthens Zero Trust MFA by cross-verifying contextual signals (GPS, IP, Wi-Fi BSSID, device posture, and TLS fingerprint) — scored for freshness, consistency, and enrichment trust — before incorporating them into authentication risk decisions, and extends detection to four network-flow-driven attack categories (Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege, Credential Attack, Infiltration) using independently trained Random Forest classifiers evaluated on a leak-free held-out split. The framework was implemented as containerised microservices and evaluated against an ablation configuration (validation layer's enrichment step disabled) and two re-implemented published baselines — Ahmadi (2025) and Phani Kumar Kanuri (2025) — on a shared, disclosed dataset combining CIC-IDS2018 network-attack traffic with real production login data from the RBA (Risk-Based Authentication) dataset (n = 1,999 sessions).

Risk scores are computed exclusively from observable signals — GPS/Wi-Fi/IP geography, device posture, TLS fingerprint, and network-flow classifier outputs — and never from the ground-truth label used for evaluation, a discipline applied identically to the proposed framework and all three baseline re-implementations to prevent target leakage. Session labels are sourced natively from the dataset rather than assigned independently of a row's content, so that the framework's measured detection rate reflects its actual signal-based capability against real attack traffic.

The proposed framework achieves 96.55% TPR at 2.65% FPR (99.37% precision, F1 = 0.979, AUC = 0.976 — the highest AUC of the four configurations compared). McNemar's test shows the proposed framework significantly outperforms Ahmadi (2025) (χ² = 1255.48, p < 10⁻²⁷⁴), Phani (2025) (χ² = 1375.44, p < 10⁻³⁰⁰), and the no-validation ablation configuration (χ² = 448.00, p < 10⁻⁹⁸). The framework leads on all six STRIDE categories; four (Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege) are tied exactly with the ablation configuration since they are driven entirely by the shared network-flow classifiers, and two (Spoofing, Tampering) reach 100% specifically because of the enrichment layer the ablation configuration lacks — isolating the validation layer's measured contribution to those two categories. The Information Disclosure figure reflects a synthetic evaluation scenario; the trained Infiltration classifier's held-out evaluation (TPR 4.15%, AUC 0.660) indicates that category remains a weak signal pending live-traffic evaluation. Median decision latency is 345ms, exceeding this study's hypothesised 50ms bound, attributable to network-flow classifier inference cost and synchronous Elasticsearch writes in the decision path. Usability impact is small (2.65% combined step-up/deny rate on legitimate sessions). Privacy-preserving mechanisms are specified as design requirements but were not verified as implemented in the running service and are reported as such, not as a measured result.

This study's contribution is a reproducible signal-quality architecture (`Qs = Fs × Cs × Es`) integrated with empirically calibrated, held-out-evaluated network-flow classification, evaluated through a controlled, disclosed head-to-head comparison against published baselines re-implemented under a shared target-leakage-free methodology. The framework is reported on its own terms, including the categories, conditions, and performance characteristics under which it does not yet perform well, alongside those in which it demonstrates a statistically significant advantage.

**Keywords:** Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA); Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA); Contextual Signal Validation; Network-Flow Detection; Random Forest Classification; SIEM Integration; STRIDE Threat Mapping; Quality Scoring; Target Leakage; Adaptive MFA.
